The four spheres

Hydrosphere

hydro = water

All of Earth's water: oceans, rivers, lakes, ice, groundwater.

Geosphere

geo = Earth

The rocky parts: crust, mantle, core, mountains, soil.

Atmosphere

atmo = vapor, air

The gases that surround Earth, such as nitrogen and oxygen.

Biosphere

bio = life

All living things: plants, animals, fungi, microbes and people.

Chapter 1 · Hydrosphere

Water, water everywhere

  • Water exists as a solid (ice), a liquid and a gas (water vapor).
  • Oceans hold salt water. Many rivers and lakes hold fresh water.
  • Groundwater is stored below Earth's surface.
  • A reservoir is a place where water collects.

The water cycle

  • 1. Evaporation. Sunlight gives energy, so liquid water becomes water vapor.
  • 2. Condensation. Cooling vapor turns into tiny droplets. Clouds are made of them.
  • 3. Precipitation. Rain, snow, sleet or hail falls back to Earth's surface.
  • 4. Collection. Gravity moves water downhill into rivers, lakes, oceans and underground.

Check 1 · Water detective

What is the difference between evaporation and condensation?
Evaporation: liquid → gas. Condensation: gas → liquid.
Why is ocean water salty?
Salts are carried from rocks into the oceans, and evaporation leaves the salt behind.
What do we call water stored underground?
Groundwater.
Chapter 2 · Geosphere

A rocky, moving planet

  • Earth has five layers: crust, upper mantle, lower mantle, outer core and inner core.
  • Crustal plates move very slowly.
  • Plate movement can cause earthquakes, mountains and volcanoes.
  • The geosphere keeps changing over time.

Three kinds of rock

  • Igneous: cooled magma or lava.
  • Sedimentary: pieces of material deposited and pressed together (it can also form in other ways).
  • Metamorphic: existing rock changed by heat and pressure.
  • The rock cycle has no fixed beginning or end. Any rock can become any other.

Check 2 · Rock challenge

Which type forms from cooling lava?
Igneous rock.
What causes an earthquake?
Energy released when rock breaks along faults makes seismic waves.
Which rock type is changed by heat and pressure?
Metamorphic rock.
Chapter 3 · Atmosphere

The blanket of air

  • Air is mostly nitrogen (about 78%) and oxygen (about 20%).
  • Most weather happens in the troposphere.
  • Air pressure decreases with altitude.
  • The ozone layer in the stratosphere helps protect living things.

Air, pressure and weather

  • Warm air rises and cool air sinks.
  • Moving air masses make wind and changing weather.
  • Low pressure goes with clouds and precipitation.
  • High pressure usually brings clearer, calmer weather.
Chapter 4 · Biosphere

Life on a living planet

  • The biosphere includes every living organism.
  • Biodiversity means the variety of species.
  • Living things interact with each other and with air, water and rocks.
  • Organisms need resources and energy from their environment.

Chapters 5–8 · Interactions

When the spheres meet

5 · Water acts on…

Weathering breaks rock down. Erosion carries sediment away. Water swaps heat and vapor with the air. Plants and animals need water to live.

6 · Land acts on…

Mountains change rainfall patterns. Land shape decides where water flows. Aquifers store groundwater. Weathered rock plus organic material makes soil.

7 · Air acts on…

Wind moves sand and sediment. Air can chemically change rock, such as rusting. Air and water trade heat and moisture. Gases support life.

8 · Life acts on…

Plants take in carbon dioxide for photosynthesis. Roots and burrowers break up rock. Decomposers return nutrients to the environment.

Exit ticket · Explain the connection (worked example)

Example: tree roots and rock
A tree root grows into a crack in a rock because it is looking for water and space. Therefore the root pushes the crack wider, the rock breaks into smaller pieces, and over time those pieces help form soil. This connects the biosphere and the geosphere.

Adding and Subtracting Negative Numbers

A slow, step-by-step guide to directed numbers (positive and negative numbers). Every idea comes with a picture and a fully worked example, so you can see exactly why the answer is what it is.

Start here: what is a negative number?

Zero is the starting line. Positive numbers are on one side of it. Negative numbers are on the other.

Positive = right of zero

More than nothing. Warmer. Above sea level. Money you have.

Negative = left of zero

Less than nothing. Colder. Below sea level. Money you owe.

Real life

SituationNumberMeaning
Temperature−5°C5 degrees below freezing
Bank balance−£20You owe the bank £20
Lift−2Two floors below the ground floor
Diver−30 m30 metres below sea level

Which is bigger?

On a number line, the number further right is bigger. So −2 is bigger than −7. Think of temperatures: −2°C is warmer than −7°C.

Solved for you: which is smaller, −7 or −3?−7 is further left of zero, so −7 is smaller. (A common slip is to think 7 is bigger than 3 so −7 is bigger. With negatives it flips.)

The two jobs a sign can do

Job 1: it names the number

In −4, the minus says "this number lives 4 steps left of zero".

Job 2: it gives an instruction

In 6 − 4, the minus says "from 6, go left 4 steps".

Almost every mistake happens when two signs sit side by side, like 5 − (−2). We will learn exactly how to deal with that.Brackets are there to keep the two signs apart so you can see which sign does which job.

1Adding

Adding is about moving along the number line.

Adding a positive number: move RIGHT.
Adding a negative number: move LEFT. (Adding a negative is the same as subtracting.)

Start on the first number. The second number tells you how far to hop. The green arrow means right (the number gets bigger). The red arrow means left (the number gets smaller).

Adding a positive

Notice: in the second one you start below zero and cross over zero. First use steps to reach 0, then use the leftover steps on the positive side.

Notice: here you do not reach zero. You stay on the negative side but you end up less negative (−4 is bigger than −6).

Adding a negative

Do not say "two negatives make a positive" here!That rule is only for a MINUS sign followed by a negative number (section 3). Here we have a PLUS and a negative number, and a negative number always sends you left. So −3 + (−4) = −7, not 7.

2Subtracting a positive number

Subtracting means "take away". On the number line it means move left.

Subtracting a positive number: move LEFT.

Why is 3 − 5 negative? You only have 3 to take from. After taking away 3 you are at zero, and you still need to take away 2 more. So you go 2 below zero. It is a very common mistake to write 3 − 5 = 2. The 2 is right but it is on the wrong side of zero.

Starting below zero and going left makes you go even lower.−5 − 3 is NOT −2. Taking away more from something already below zero makes it more negative. Think: it is −5°C and it gets 3 degrees colder. Now it is −8°C.

3Subtracting a negative number

This is the tricky one, so here are three different ways to see why it works. Pick the one that makes sense to you.

Subtracting a negative number is the same as ADDING. Move RIGHT.Two minus signs in a row (a minus, then a negative number) turn into a plus.

Way 1: Debt

Say you owe £5. That is −5. Someone takes away £3 of your debt. That is − (−3).

Now you only owe £2. Your money went UP from −5 to −2.

−5 − (−3) = −2

Way 2: Temperature

It is 2°C. A heater removes 4 degrees of "cold" (that is −4 degrees of cold taken away).

Taking away cold makes it warmer: 6°C.

2 − (−4) = 6

Way 3: The pattern

Look at the table below and watch what happens as the number being subtracted goes down.

Way 3 in full: the pattern never breaks

Each row, the number we subtract goes down by 1, and the answer goes up by 1. The pattern keeps going past zero, so 3 − (−1) has to be 4, 3 − (−2) has to be 5, and so on.

4The Sign Squeeze

When two signs sit side by side, squeeze them into one sign. Then the question is just a plain move left or right.

5One method for every question

Use these four steps every time. Do not skip step 2.

Step 1: Spot the start

The first number (with its sign) is where you stand on the number line.

Step 2: Squeeze the signs

If two signs touch, squeeze them into one (same signs → +, different signs → −).

Step 3: Choose the direction

+ means move right. − means move left.

Step 4: Move and check

Count the steps. Remember zero is a stop on the way, not a wall.

Full worked example

Question: −4 − (−9)

Sense check: bigger or smaller?

Before you write your answer, ask: should it be bigger or smaller than the start? If your answer goes the wrong way, something went wrong.

You do thisExampleAnswer is…
Add a positive−3 + 5 = 2bigger than the start
Add a negative−3 + (−5) = −8smaller than the start
Subtract a positive−3 − 5 = −8smaller than the start
Subtract a negative−3 − (−5) = 2bigger than the start

Quick shortcut (once you are confident)

Same signs: add and keep the sign

−4 − 5 → both are "down", so add 4 + 5 = 9 and keep the minus: −9.

Different signs: subtract and take the sign of the bigger one

−9 + 4 → 9 − 4 = 5, and 9 is the negative one, so −5.
